\chapter{Dyson Series and Response Functions (Matter Side)}
\label{app:response_functions}

This appendix derives explicit, causal expressions for the $n$-th order Polarisation. It keeps the full tensorial formulation where the polarisation $\boldsymbol P^{(n)}(t)$ is a vector and the response $R^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta_1\cdots\beta_n}$ is a rank-$(n{+}1)$ tensor. Starting from a driven master equation for the reduced density operator $\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)$, the field dependence is isolated in the interaction picture and expanded in powers of the field. This leads to response functions, which capture the intrinsic material dynamics, and to convolution formulas for $P^{(n)}_{\alpha}$ that separate material properties from pulse shapes.

\section{Driven Master Equation}
\label{app:sec:dyson_response}

The starting point is a driven open-system equation of motion for the reduced density operator $\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)$ of the material system after tracing out the environment,
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}
	= -\mathrm{i}\,\mathcal{K}[\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)]
	\quad + \mathrm{i}\,\mathcal{V}_{\beta}[\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)]\,E_{\beta}(t),
	\label{eq:app_master_equation_with_field}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{K}$ is the field-free reduced generator obtained after eliminating the environment. It contains the Hamiltonian evolution of the system as well as dissipation and dephasing effects, as in Redfield or Lindblad theory (cf.~\autoref{sec:oqs_Redfield_eq}). The driving field $\boldsymbol{E}(t)$ enters through its Cartesian components $E_{\beta}(t)$ and the corresponding dipole superoperators
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{V}_{\beta}[\hat{A}] \equiv [\hat{\mu}_{\beta},\hat{A}],
	\label{eq:app_dipole_superoperator}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{\mu}_{\beta}$ the Cartesian components of the dipole operator $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}$. Throughout this appendix repeated Cartesian indices are summed over (Einstein convention).

The commutator in~\autoref{eq:app_dipole_superoperator} makes explicit that each interaction has a left and a right action on the density operator. This is the algebraic origin of Liouville-space pathways: whether $\hat{\mu}_{\beta}$ acts on the ket or on the bra determines which coherences or populations are created at each interaction. The commutator introduces a relative minus sign between the two actions.

The field-free propagator is defined as
\begin{equation*}
	\mathcal{U}_0(t) \equiv \exp(-\mathrm{i}\,\mathcal{K}t),
\end{equation*}
so that in the absence of the field the state evolves as
\begin{equation*}
	\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)=\mathcal{U}_0(t-t_0)[\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t_0)].
\end{equation*}

\section{Interaction Picture Formulation}
\label{app:sec:interaction_picture_K}

To isolate the field-driven part of the evolution, introduce an interaction picture with respect to the field-free generator $\mathcal{K}$. Define the interaction-picture density operator by
\begin{equation}
	\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S,I}}(t)
	\equiv \mathcal{U}_0\!\bigl(-(t-t_0)\bigr)[\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)],
	\label{eq:app_rhoI_def}
\end{equation}
and the corresponding time-dependent dipole superoperators by
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm I,\beta}(t)
	\equiv \mathcal{U}_0\!\bigl(-(t-t_0)\bigr)\,\mathcal{V}_{\beta}\,\mathcal{U}_0(t-t_0).
	\label{eq:dipole_super_op_interaction_pic}
\end{equation}
By differentiating \autoref{eq:app_rhoI_def} with respect to time, inserting the master equation \autoref{eq:app_master_equation_with_field}, and using that $\mathcal{U}_0$ commutes with $\mathcal{K}$, the equation of motion reduces to
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S,I}}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}
	= \mathrm{i}\,\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm I,\beta}(t)[\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S,I}}(t)]\,E_{\beta}(t).
	\label{eq:app_rhoI_eom}
\end{equation}
This form is exact for the reduced system. The field-free dynamics have been absorbed into the picture transformation and into the explicit time dependence of $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm I,\beta}(t)$. The only remaining term on the right-hand side is the light--matter coupling, which makes \autoref{eq:app_rhoI_eom} a convenient starting point for perturbation theory in the field strength.

\section{Dyson Series Expansion}
\label{app:sec:dyson_series}

Convert \autoref{eq:app_rhoI_eom} into an integral equation by integrating from an initial time $t_0$ to $t$,
\begin{equation}
	\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S,I}}(t)
	= \hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S,I}}(t_0)
	\quad + \mathrm{i}\int_{t_0}^{t}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm I,\beta}(\tau)[\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S,I}}(\tau)]\,E_{\beta}(\tau).
	\label{eq:app_rhoI_integral}
\end{equation}
The initial condition is the same in both pictures,
$\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S,I}}(t_0)=\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t_0)$.

To extract contributions of definite order in the field, substitute \autoref{eq:app_rhoI_integral} back into itself iteratively. After $n$ substitutions, collect all terms containing $n$ factors of $E_{\beta}$. This produces the Dyson series (cf.~\autoref{subsec:oqs_interaction_picture}), which is an infinite perturbation expansion in the field strength,
\begin{equation}
	\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S,I}}(t)
	= \Biggl[
	\mathds{1}
	+ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (\mathrm{i})^n
	\int_{t_0}^{t}\!\mathrm{d}\tau_1
	\int_{t_0}^{\tau_1}\!\mathrm{d}\tau_2\,\cdots
	\int_{t_0}^{\tau_{n-1}}\!\mathrm{d}\tau_n\,
	\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm I,\beta_1}(\tau_1)\cdots\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm I,\beta_n}(\tau_n)\,
	E_{\beta_1}(\tau_1)\cdots E_{\beta_n}(\tau_n)
	\Biggr][\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t_0)].
	\label{eq:app_dyson}
\end{equation}
The nested limits $t_0<\tau_n<\cdots<\tau_2<\tau_1<t$ enforce time ordering and therefore causality.
This equation is useful for nonlinear spectroscopy because it isolates the $n$-th order term that can be selected experimentally.

\section{Polarisation as a Perturbation Series}
\label{app:sec:polarisation_series}

The macroscopic polarisation is defined as the expectation value of the dipole operator (cf.~\autoref{eq:spctr_P_def_spectroscopy}),
\begin{equation*}
	P_\alpha(t)
	= \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{S}}\!\left\{\hat{\mu}_\alpha\,\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t)\right\}.
\end{equation*}
Using the interaction-picture representation of the state and cyclicity of the trace, it can be written as
\begin{equation*}
	P_\alpha(t)
	= \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{S}}\!\left\{\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm I,\alpha}(t)\,\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S,I}}(t)\right\}
	= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P^{(n)}_\alpha(t),
\end{equation*}
where $\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm I,\alpha}(t)$ is the dipole operator evolved under the field-free dynamics. In Liouville-space notation this is the adjoint action of the field-free propagator on the observable,
\begin{equation*}
	\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm I,\alpha}(t)=\mathcal{U}_0^{\dagger}(t-t_0)[\hat{\mu}_\alpha].
\end{equation*}
The $n$-th order contribution is
\begin{equation*}
	P^{(n)}_\alpha(t) = \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{S}}\!\left\{\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm I,\alpha}(t)\,\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S,I}}^{(n)}(t)\right\},
\end{equation*}
with $\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S,I}}^{(n)}$ the term containing $n$ factors of the field.

Substituting the Dyson expansion \autoref{eq:app_dyson} and extracting the $n$-th order term yields an expression that is naturally written using the field-free propagator $\mathcal{U}_0$ and the Schr\"odinger-picture superoperators $\mathcal{V}_\beta$. Repeatedly insert the definition \autoref{eq:dipole_super_op_interaction_pic} into the ordered product
$\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm I,\beta_1}(\tau_1)\cdots \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm I,\beta_n}(\tau_n)$,
use the group property $\mathcal{U}_0(a)\mathcal{U}_0(b)=\mathcal{U}_0(a+b)$ to combine adjacent propagators, and move the remaining picture transformations within the trace using cyclicity. This produces free-propagation intervals between successive interaction events and the final interval between the last interaction and the measurement time. The result is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
P^{(n)}_\alpha(t)
&= (\mathrm{i})^n
\int_{t_0}^{t}\!\mathrm{d}\tau^{(1)}\cdots
\int_{t_0}^{\tau^{(n-1)}}\!\mathrm{d}\tau^{(n)}
\\
&\quad \times
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{S}}\Bigl\{
\hat{\mu}_\alpha
\,\mathcal{U}_0(t-\tau^{(1)})
\,\mathcal{V}_{\beta_1}
\,\mathcal{U}_0(\tau^{(1)}-\tau^{(2)})
\\
&\qquad
\,\mathcal{V}_{\beta_2}
\cdots
\mathcal{V}_{\beta_n}
\,\mathcal{U}_0(\tau^{(n)}-t_0)
[\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t_0)]
\Bigr\}
\\
&\quad \times
\prod_{j=1}^{n} E_{\beta_j}(\tau^{(j)}).
\end{split}
\label{eq:app_Pn_explicit}
\end{equation}
Reading from right to left inside the trace, the system starts in the initial state $\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t_0)$, undergoes $n$ alternating interactions (via $\mathcal{V}_{\beta_j}$) and field-free evolutions (via $\mathcal{U}_0$), and is finally probed through the dipole operator $\hat{\mu}_\alpha$. This sequence underlies the usual Liouville-space pathway picture.

\section{Definition of the Response Function}
\label{app:sec:response_function_definition}

The expression \autoref{eq:app_Pn_explicit} is written in terms of absolute interaction times $\tau^{(j)}$. For spectroscopy it is often more convenient to use nonnegative delay intervals between successive events. Define
\begin{equation*}
\tau_n = t-\tau^{(1)},\qquad
\tau_{n-j}=
\tau^{(j)}-\tau^{(j+1)}\ (j=1,\ldots,n-1),
\end{equation*}
so that each $\tau_j\ge 0$ is an interval between two adjacent interactions, and $\tau_n$ is the interval between the last interaction and the measurement at time $t$. Inverting these relations gives
\begin{equation}
\tau^{(n)} = t-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tau_k,\qquad
\tau^{(n-1)} = t-\sum_{k=2}^{n}\tau_k,\qquad \ldots,\qquad
\tau^{(1)} = t-\tau_n.
	\label{eq:app_change_of_variables}
\end{equation}
The Jacobian of the transformation is unity. In the limit $t_0\to-\infty$, the nested integration region over absolute times maps to independent integrals over $\tau_j\in[0,\infty)$. This change of variables is conceptually depicted in~\autoref{fig:app_time_variables_absolute_vs_intervals}.

Substitute \autoref{eq:app_change_of_variables} into \autoref{eq:app_Pn_explicit} and assume the system starts in equilibrium, $\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{S}}(t_0)=\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{eq}}$, at $t_0\to -\infty$. The initial evolution is then trivial because $\mathcal{U}_0(t)[\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{eq}}]=\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{eq}}$. The $n$-th order polarisation becomes
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
P^{(n)}_\alpha(t)
&= \int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}\tau_n\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}\tau_1\,
R^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta_1\cdots\beta_n}(\tau_n,\ldots,\tau_1)
\\
&\quad \times
E_{\beta_1}(t-\tau_n)\,
E_{\beta_2}(t-\tau_n-\tau_{n-1})\cdots
E_{\beta_n}\!\left(t-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tau_k\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:app_Pn_convolution}
\end{equation}
This formula separates the material dynamics from the experimental pulse shapes. The response tensor $R^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta_1\cdots\beta_n}$ is the field-independent factor,
\begin{equation*}
	R^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta_1\cdots\beta_n}(\tau_n,\ldots,\tau_1)
	\equiv (\mathrm{i})^n\,\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{S}}\Bigl\{\hat{\mu}_\alpha\,\mathcal{U}_0(\tau_n)\,\mathcal{V}_{\beta_n}\,\mathcal{U}_0(\tau_{n-1})\,\mathcal{V}_{\beta_{n-1}}\cdots\mathcal{U}_0(\tau_1)\,\mathcal{V}_{\beta_1}[\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{eq}}]\Bigr\}.
\end{equation*}
By construction, $R^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta_1\cdots\beta_n}(\tau_n,\ldots,\tau_1)$ describes the intrinsic response of the system to $n$ successive light--matter interactions separated by nonnegative delay intervals. Causality is explicit: the response vanishes whenever any $\tau_j<0$, and \autoref{eq:app_Pn_convolution} only involves field values at times earlier than $t$.

\section{Fixed-Polarisation Projection Used in This Thesis}
\label{app:sec:scalar_projection_note}

The derivation above is fully polarisation-resolved. In the numerical simulations reported in this thesis, all pulses are taken linearly polarised along a fixed laboratory direction $\boldsymbol e_{\mathrm{in}}$, and the detected signal is the component along $\boldsymbol e_{\mathrm{out}}$ (typically $\boldsymbol e_{\mathrm{out}}=\boldsymbol e_{\mathrm{in}}$). Because the simulations use a collinear setup with one fixed polarisation axis for excitation and readout, this projection is used throughout. Accordingly,
\begin{equation*}
	E_\beta(t)=e_{\mathrm{in},\beta}\,E(t),
	\qquad
	P(t)=e_{\mathrm{out},\alpha}\,P_\alpha(t),
\end{equation*}
so that the tensor response contracts to the scalar quantity
\begin{equation*}
	R^{(n)} \equiv
	e_{\mathrm{out},\alpha}\,R^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta_1\cdots\beta_n}\,
	e_{\mathrm{in},\beta_1}\cdots e_{\mathrm{in},\beta_n}.
\end{equation*}
In this fixed-polarisation setting, the equations used in the main text are
\begin{equation}
	P^{(n)}(t)
	= \int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}\tau_n\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}\tau_1\,
	R^{(n)}(\tau_n,\ldots,\tau_1)\,
	E(t-\tau_n)\,E(t-\tau_n-\tau_{n-1})\cdots
	E\!\Bigl(t-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tau_k\Bigr),
	\label{eq:app_Pn_convolution_scalar}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	R^{(n)}(\tau_n,\ldots,\tau_1)
	\equiv (\mathrm{i})^n\,\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{S}}\Bigl\{\hat{\mu}\,\mathcal{U}_0(\tau_n)\,\mathcal{V}\,\mathcal{U}_0(\tau_{n-1})\,\mathcal{V}\cdots\mathcal{V}\,\mathcal{U}_0(\tau_1)\,\mathcal{V}[\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{eq}}]\Bigr\}.
	\label{eq:app_Sn_def_scalar}
\end{equation}
Whenever response functions are used in the main text, indices are suppressed and $P^{(n)}$ and $R^{(n)}$ refer to these fixed-polarisation scalar projections. This is consistent with the numerical workflow, which uses a collinear setup (see~\autoref{sec:spctr_phase_cycling}) and therefore a fixed laboratory polarisation orientation.

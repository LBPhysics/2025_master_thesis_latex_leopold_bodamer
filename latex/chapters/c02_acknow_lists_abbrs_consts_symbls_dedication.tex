\subsection*{Notation and conventions}

Throughout this thesis, the following conventions are used:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Vectors in real space are denoted by bold symbols, e.g.\ $\boldsymbol{E}$, $\boldsymbol{P}$, $\boldsymbol{B}$, $\boldsymbol{k}$, $\boldsymbol{d}$, $\boldsymbol{e}$.
	\item Operators on Hilbert spaces carry a hat, e.g.\ $\hat{H}$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}$ for the dipole operator, and density operators are written as $\hat{\rho}$. To distinguish representations (Schr\"odinger, interaction, Heisenberg), subscripts are used; in particular $\hat{O}_I(t)$ denotes an interaction-picture operator. To avoid ambiguity with the interaction-picture subscript, the interaction Hamiltonian is denoted $\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}}$.
	\item Superoperators acting on density operators are written in calligraphic font, e.g.\ $\mathcal{U}_0$, $\mathcal{L}$, $\mathcal{M}$.
	\item For a fixed laboratory polarisation direction $\boldsymbol{e}$, the vector field is often projected onto this axis and a scalar envelope is used,
	\[
		\varepsilon(t) = \boldsymbol{e}\cdot\boldsymbol{E}(t),
	\],
	and similarly with the corresponding dipole component $\hat{\mu}=\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}$. In such cases the vector character is understood and suppressed in the notation.
	\item Transition dipole moments are written as vectors $\boldsymbol{d}$; their magnitudes are denoted $d = |\boldsymbol{d}|$ where needed.
	\item The symbols $\Re$ and $\Im$ denote the real and imaginary parts of a complex quantity, respectively.

\end{itemize}

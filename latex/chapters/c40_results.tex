This chapter combines the open-system framework of~\autoref{chapter:oqs_open_quantum_systems} with the spectroscopy workflow of~\autoref{chapter:spctr_spectroscopy}. Its central aim is to reproduce selected spectra from Papers~I and II inside one consistent QuTiP-based workflow~\cite{mancaletal2006twodimensionalopticalthreepulse,pisliakovetal2006twodimensionalopticalthreepulse}. This reformulation is motivated by generality: once the spectroscopy pipeline is written in terms of QuTiP Hamiltonians, operators, and solver backends, the same numerical machinery can be extended from the paper-specific monomer and dimer equations to more general Hamiltonians, larger systems, and microscopic bath models without relying on explicit analytical equations of motion. In this chapter, the explicit paper equations define the calibration reference. The monomer is then interpreted through a Lindblad baseline and a Redfield bath extension, while the dimer is calibrated directly against the QuTiP Redfield implementation. The complete paper-equation comparison atlas is collected in~\autoref{app:paper_eqs_reference_atlas}.

\section{Monomer}
\label{sec:results_monomer}

The discussion starts with the monomer because it is the simplest system in which the complete spectroscopy workflow can be read almost without ambiguity. In the strict two-level limit, the third-order signal contains only ground-state bleach and stimulated-emission contributions. Excited-state absorption is absent because no higher excited manifold is available, as discussed in~\autoref{sec:spctr_two_level_system_linear_response} and~\autoref{sec:spctr_two_level_third_order_pathways}. This makes the monomer the natural place to organize the results in the same order in which the signal is constructed in~\autoref{sec:spctr_2D_construction_formal}: first the time-domain response, then its Fourier transform, then the full $(t_{\mathrm{coh}},t_{\mathrm{det}})$ maps, and finally the 2D spectra in $(\omega_{\mathrm{coh}},\omega_{\mathrm{det}})$ space.

\subsection{Model and equations of motion}
\label{subsec:results_monomer_model_eom}

The monomer is the simplest case in which the full three-pulse workflow can be inspected. Its field-free Hamiltonian is, cf.~\cref{eq:spctr_tls_hamiltonian,eq:spctr_tls_dipole},
\begin{equation*}
\hat{H}_{0}^{(\mathrm{mon})}=\omega_{eg}\ket{e}\!\bra{e},
\end{equation*}
and the dipole operator is
\begin{equation*}
\hat{\mu}^{(\mathrm{mon})}=\mu_{ge}\ket{g}\!\bra{e}+\mu_{eg}\ket{e}\!\bra{g}.
\end{equation*}
The published phenomenology of Paper~I is characterised by a population decay rate $\gamma$ and a pure dephasing rate $\gamma_{\varphi}$, with $T_1=\gamma^{-1}$ and $T_2^{-1}=\gamma/2+\gamma_{\varphi}$. In the QuTiP implementation used here, the same phenomenology is written in Lindblad form with
\begin{equation*}
\hat{L}_{\varphi}=\sqrt{2\gamma_{\varphi}}\ket{e}\!\bra{e},
\qquad
\hat{L}_{\downarrow}=\sqrt{\gamma_{\downarrow}}\ket{g}\!\bra{e},
\qquad
\hat{L}_{\uparrow}=\sqrt{\gamma_{\uparrow}}\ket{e}\!\bra{g}.
\end{equation*}
The identification with the notation of Paper~I is therefore $\gamma_{\downarrow}\equiv\gamma$. The printed paper phenomenology does not introduce an upward thermal excitation channel, so in the strict calibration against the paper one sets $\gamma_{\uparrow}=0$. Physically this corresponds to the zero-temperature limit of the phenomenological bath, where detailed balance suppresses upward transitions and only relaxation from $\ket{e}$ to $\ket{g}$ remains. Keeping $\gamma_{\uparrow}$ explicit is nevertheless useful because it shows how the phenomenological two-level model would be extended to a finite-temperature bath even before switching to a microscopic Redfield description. The explicit rotating-wave equations used by the phenomenological calibration backend are collected in~\autoref{app:sec:paper_eqs_explicit_eoms}; in the main text they serve as the reference against which the QuTiP implementations are judged.

For real samples, the measured response reflects an ensemble of local environments rather than a single perfectly identical monomer, so the spectra are evaluated over many static-disorder realisations, see~\autoref{spctr:sec:macroscopic_polarisation}. The transition frequency is sampled from the Gaussian distribution introduced in~\autoref{eq:gaussian_broadening}. The spectra are then obtained from the disorder-averaged signal defined in~\autoref{eq:averaged_response}. The parameter $\Delta_{\mathrm{inh}}$ denotes the corresponding full width at half maximum of the distribution. In the homogeneous case, $\Delta_{\mathrm{inh}}=0$, all realisations share the same transition frequency and the linewidth is determined by dynamical dephasing and relaxation. In the inhomogeneous case, static disorder adds a spread of transition frequencies across the ensemble, exactly the distinction emphasized already in~\autoref{sec:spctr_spectroscopy_motivation}.

All monomer runs discussed in the main text use $\tilde{\nu}_{eg}=\SI{16000}{\per\centi\metre}$, $\mu_{ge}=1$, resonant excitation, and pulse amplitudes $(0.002,0.002,0.002)$. In units with $\hbar=1$, the quantity $\mu_{eg}E(t)$ has units of inverse time and corresponds to the instantaneous Rabi frequency, while the choice $\mu_{ge}=1$ fixes the field-amplitude scale used throughout this section. For the phenomenological benchmark, the fitted rates are $\gamma_{\varphi}=0.01~\si{\per\femto\second}$, $\gamma_{\downarrow}=1/300~\si{\per\femto\second}$, and $\gamma_{\uparrow}=0$. Static disorder, when included, uses $\Delta_{\mathrm{inh}}=\SI{200}{\per\centi\metre}$. The appendix additionally documents a supplementary Fig.~4 sensitivity set with stronger pulses $(0.005,0.005,0.005)$.

This is the first direct place to see the difference between the rephasing and nonrephasing branches discussed in~\autoref{subsec:spctr_rephasing_nonrephasing}. The physical message of~\autoref{fig:results_monomer_1d_time_hom_inhom} is the classic photon-echo logic. In the homogeneous ensemble, all monomers oscillate at the same transition frequency, so there is no ensemble dephasing that could later be refocused. Consequently, the rephasing trace decays away from the prompt response and does not show a delayed maximum near $t_{\mathrm{det}}\approx t_{\mathrm{coh}}$. Once static disorder is introduced, the rephasing signal develops exactly such a delayed peak: the phase reversal of the rephasing branch refocuses the ensemble frequency spread and creates the photon echo. The nonrephasing branch does not refocus disorder and therefore stays concentrated at short detection times, in agreement with~\autoref{subsec:spctr_rephasing_nonrephasing}. The raw exported peak ratio changes from $R/NR=1.00$ in the homogeneous slice to $1.45$ in the inhomogeneous one, so the rephasing branch already becomes the larger of the two before any Fourier transformation is applied.

In the homogeneous branch, the spectral weight stays concentrated in one compact resonance around $\tilde{\nu}_{eg}=\SI{16000}{\per\centi\metre}$, together with the expected dispersive partner in the imaginary part. In the inhomogeneous branch, the envelope broadens around the same central transition frequency because the ensemble average collects many slightly shifted single-molecule responses, exactly the mechanism discussed in~\autoref{sec:spctr_spectroscopy_motivation}. \subsection{2D time-domain response}
\label{subsec:results_monomer_2d_time}

With the 1D calibration in place, the full time-domain signal $E_{\mathrm{R/NR}}(t_{\mathrm{coh}},t_{\mathrm{wait}},t_{\mathrm{det}})$ makes visible which structures are already present before the double Fourier transform. This representation is especially instructive for the monomer because it shows directly how the echo ridge appears in the rephasing channel and why it is absent from the nonrephasing channel. The appendix contains the matching \texttt{paper\_eqs} exports; the main text now focuses on the calibrated Lindblad series.

For the homogeneous monomer in~\autoref{fig:results_monomer_2d_time_hom}, both rephasing and nonrephasing intensity remain concentrated near the origin and decay with increasing $t_{\mathrm{coh}}$ and $t_{\mathrm{det}}$. The absence of a pronounced ridge along $t_{\mathrm{det}}\approx t_{\mathrm{coh}}$ is the time-domain counterpart of the missing photon echo already seen in~\autoref{fig:results_monomer_1d_time_hom_inhom}. The main waiting-time dependence is not a new internal monomer dynamics, but the finite-pulse regime itself: $T=\SI{0}{\femto\second}$ and $T=\SI{20}{\femto\second}$ are still affected by pulse overlap, while by $T=\SI{40}{\femto\second}$ the shape has essentially settled. The inhomogeneous case in~\autoref{fig:results_monomer_2d_time_inhom} changes specifically in the rephasing branch. There the signal acquires an elongated structure roughly following $t_{\mathrm{det}}\sim t_{\mathrm{coh}}$, which is the 2D time-domain precursor of both the delayed photon echo and the diagonal elongation seen after Fourier transformation. The nonrephasing branch stays more localized and does not show comparable refocusing, exactly as expected from the pathway discussion in~\autoref{subsec:spctr_rephasing_nonrephasing}. Once again, the visible $T$-dependence is weak once the pulse-overlap regime has been left, already foreshadowing that the monomer has no genuine waiting-time redistribution dynamics in this static model. The later peak-ratio summary makes an important limitation explicit: after $T=\SI{20}{\femto\second}$, the 2D time-domain maxima of the two branches are already very close, even though the rephasing ridge remains visibly much more structured. %\todoimp{In this representation, the key physics is therefore more geometric than purely peak-amplitude based. -> satz ist komisch}

\subsection{2D frequency-domain response}
\label{subsec:results_monomer_2d_freq}

The double Fourier transform of the time-domain maps produces the rephasing and nonrephasing spectra of~\cref{eq:SR_def,eq:SNR_def}. Their absorptive combination, defined in~\autoref{eq:Sab_def}, is the cleanest representation for physical interpretation because it suppresses the dispersive distortions present in the individual branches. The corresponding \texttt{paper\_eqs} panels are collected in~\autoref{app:paper_eqs_reference_atlas}, so the main chapter now focuses on the Lindblad spectra that carry the physical interpretation.

The homogeneous series in~\autoref{fig:results_monomer_2d_hom_lindblad} reproduces the compact, nearly symmetric star-like monomer profile emphasized in Paper~I. Because only a single transition contributes, there are no cross peaks, no excited-state-absorption features, and no population transfer signatures during $t_{\mathrm{wait}}$. The short-$T$ distortions are therefore best understood as finite-pulse overlap effects of the same type discussed in Paper~I: $T=\SI{0}{\femto\second}$ shows the strongest phase-twist behaviour, $T=\SI{20}{\femto\second}$ is intermediate, and by $T=\SI{40}{\femto\second}$ the line shape has essentially reached the final homogeneous profile. The corresponding frequency-domain branch ratios likewise collapse back to unity once the pulse-overlap regime has passed.

The inhomogeneous series in~\autoref{fig:results_monomer_2d_inhom_lindblad} develops the diagonal elongation that is the central 2D signature of static disorder. This is precisely the type of homogeneous-versus-inhomogeneous separation described in~\autoref{sec:spctr_spectroscopy_motivation}: the spectrum can be understood as a superposition of many homogeneous spectra shifted along the diagonal by realization-dependent transition frequencies. The diagonal width therefore grows with disorder, whereas the antidiagonal width continues to reflect the homogeneous dephasing. No cross peaks appear because the monomer has neither a second excited transition nor a transfer channel. As in the homogeneous case, the visible waiting-time evolution is largely confined to the pulse-overlap regime; once that has passed, the line shape changes little and mainly loses amplitude. Unlike in the 2D time-domain maps, however, the frequency-domain peak ratios remain clearly above unity even after pulse overlap, so the rephasing branch stays visibly dominant in the inhomogeneous spectra.

Taken together, the 2D time- and frequency-domain views show the same physics in complementary representations: the echo ridge in the rephasing time-domain signal becomes the diagonal elongation of the inhomogeneous absorptive spectrum after double Fourier transformation.

To map these visual impressions onto a quantitative summary,~\autoref{tab:results_monomer_branch_ratios} lists the ratios of the raw exported branch maxima for the Lindblad figures discussed above. The 1D rows refer to~\autoref{fig:results_monomer_1d_time_hom_inhom} and~\autoref{fig:results_monomer_1d_freq_hom_inhom}; the 2D rows follow the waiting-time panels in~\autoref{fig:results_monomer_2d_time_hom}, \autoref{fig:results_monomer_2d_time_inhom}, \autoref{fig:results_monomer_2d_hom_lindblad}, and~\autoref{fig:results_monomer_2d_inhom_lindblad}. Because the table uses ratios of raw rephasing and nonrephasing peaks, it is insensitive to the plotting normalization applied within each figure. Two conclusions follow immediately. First, static disorder never makes the nonrephasing branch dominant in the displayed monomer results: whenever the two branches differ appreciably, the rephasing branch is larger. Second, the usefulness of a peak-ratio diagnostic depends on representation. In 1D traces and especially in 2D frequency space, the ratios track the disorder physics clearly. In the 2D time domain, by contrast, the decisive difference lies more in the extended shape of the rephasing ridge than in a much larger single maximum.

For the question of which branch contributes more strongly to the total monomer signal, peak heights are therefore a useful compact diagnostic but not a complete answer. They work well for the 1D slices and especially for the 2D frequency-domain spectra, where the echo-induced refocusing becomes a concentrated spectral maximum. In the 2D time domain, however, a true total-weight comparison would be better based on an integral or norm over the full branch map. Among the branch maxima reported here, the numerically largest raw value is the homogeneous 2D frequency-domain rephasing peak at $T=\SI{0}{\femto\second}$, $\max|\tilde{S}_{\mathrm{R}}|=1.17\times10^{-1}$. This should still not be compared directly to the time-domain maxima, because the Fourier transformation changes both the units and the way the signal weight is distributed.

\subsection{Monomer summary}
\label{subsec:results_monomer_summary}

The monomer results are deliberately organized in the same order as the signal construction itself. The main text keeps only the simplest homogeneous paper-vs-Lindblad calibration in 1D time and 1D frequency space; all further \texttt{paper\_eqs} mirrors are deferred to~\autoref{app:paper_eqs_reference_atlas}. Within the Lindblad results, the 1D time-domain traces show the basic photon-echo logic: no delayed echo in the homogeneous case, but a clear rephasing echo once static disorder is introduced. The 1D frequency-domain traces then show the corresponding line-shape contrast, while the 2D time-domain maps make the echo ridge visible before Fourier transformation and the 2D frequency-domain spectra turn this into the compact homogeneous peak and the diagonally elongated inhomogeneous peak familiar from Paper~I.

The branch-ratio summary sharpens that picture. Static disorder makes the rephasing contribution systematically stronger than the nonrephasing one, but the clarity of that statement depends on representation. Peak heights are already useful in 1D and become especially informative in 2D frequency space, where the inhomogeneous rephasing branch remains about $2.7$ times larger than the nonrephasing branch after pulse overlap. In 2D time space, by contrast, the decisive signature is the geometry of the echo ridge, not a much larger peak amplitude. Because the monomer has no cross peaks and no genuine waiting-time redistribution dynamics, it serves as the clean calibration case in which broadening physics can be separated from pulse-overlap effects before moving to more structured systems.

\section{Dimer}
\label{sec:results_dimer}

\subsection{Model and calibration strategy}
\label{subsec:results_dimer_model_eom}

The dimer is the minimal model in which cross peaks, waiting-time beats, and adjacent-manifold transitions appear together. In the localized site basis $\{\ket{g},\ket{A},\ket{B},\ket{f}\equiv\ket{AB}\}$, consistent with the dimer conventions used throughout this thesis, the field-free Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation*}
\hat{H}_{0}^{(\mathrm{dim})}
=
\omega_{A}\ket{A}\!\bra{A}
+
\omega_{B}\ket{B}\!\bra{B}
+
J\bigl(\ket{A}\!\bra{B}+\ket{B}\!\bra{A}\bigr)
+
(\omega_{A}+\omega_{B})\ket{f}\!\bra{f},
\end{equation*}
Diagonalizing only the single-exciton block gives the exciton representation used in Paper~II and in the regenerated \texttt{paper\_eqs} backend: \(\ket{0}=\ket{g}\), \(\ket{3}=\ket{AB}\), and the states \(\ket{1}\) and \(\ket{2}\) are the two \(J\)-mixed superpositions of \(\ket{A}\) and \(\ket{B}\). \autoref{fig:results_dimer_basis_map} summarizes that notation bridge so that the localized Hamiltonian and the numbered Paper-II equations can be read as the same model in two bases. The dipole operator then connects adjacent manifolds in either representation. The regenerated dimer archive used in this thesis follows the same basic Paper-II control logic~\cite{pisliakovetal2006twodimensionalopticalthreepulse}: a $T=0$ uncoupled-versus-coupled comparison and a waiting-time series for the coupled dimer. In the refreshed run set mirrored here, the pulse amplitudes are $(0.002,0.002,0.002)$. The inhomogeneous Fig.~4 sequence uses $\Delta_{\mathrm{inh}}=\SI{200}{\per\centi\metre}$ with $N_{\mathrm{inhom}}=10$, while the Fig.~6 companion sequence is homogeneous with $\Delta_{\mathrm{inh}}=0$ and $N_{\mathrm{inhom}}=1$.

\subsection{Amplitude balance in the regenerated dimer archive}
\label{subsec:results_dimer_amplitudes}

The regenerated dimer plot logs allow the same raw branch-max comparison used above for the monomer, but the interpretation is less straightforward. For the current Fig.~3 control pair in~\autoref{fig:results_dimer_calibration}, the thesis therefore keeps the discussion qualitative and structural. The quantitative amplitude summary below is instead based on the refreshed waiting-time archive in~\autoref{app:paper_eqs_reference_atlas}, because those runs export standardized raw maxima directly in the plotting logs. As in the monomer case, the ratios are insensitive to the per-figure plotting normalization. Unlike in the monomer, however, the largest local maximum is no longer tied to one simple echo-versus-no-echo contrast. In the coupled dimer, diagonal peaks, cross peaks, and doubly excited-manifold contributions can all compete for the dominant maximum.

To make that point explicit,~\autoref{tab:results_dimer_branch_ratios} lists the no-apodization ratios extracted from the raw dimer plot logs. The Fig.~4 rows follow the left/right appendix panels in \autoref{fig:app_dimer_fig4_time_t0_t16} and \autoref{fig:app_dimer_fig4_freq_t0_t16}, then \autoref{fig:app_dimer_fig4_time_t30_t46} and \autoref{fig:app_dimer_fig4_freq_t30_t46}, then \autoref{fig:app_dimer_fig4_time_t62_t108} and \autoref{fig:app_dimer_fig4_freq_t62_t108}, and finally \autoref{fig:app_dimer_fig4_time_t140_t310} and \autoref{fig:app_dimer_fig4_freq_t140_t310}. The Fig.~6 rows likewise follow the left/right panels of \autoref{fig:app_dimer_fig6_time} and \autoref{fig:app_dimer_fig6_freq}. Three trends stand out. First, the inhomogeneous Fig.~4 series keeps the frequency-domain rephasing branch larger for all displayed waiting times, with \(R/NR\) values between \(1.29\) and \(4.83\). This is still qualitatively consistent with the refocusing logic familiar from the monomer: static disorder gives the rephasing branch a more concentrated spectral maximum. Second, the time-domain ratios of the same Fig.~4 series are often much closer to unity, except at \(T=\SI{16}{\femto\second}\) and \(T=\SI{46}{\femto\second}\). As in the monomer, this suggests that peak-height comparisons are more informative in frequency space than in the full \((t_{\mathrm{coh}},t_{\mathrm{det}})\) maps. Third, the homogeneous Fig.~6 companion sequence shows a genuine difference from the monomer: at \(T=\SI{62}{\femto\second}\), the nonrephasing branch becomes the larger one in both domains, with \(R/NR=0.97\) in time space and \(0.64\) in frequency space.

A plausible reason for this difference is that the dimer has no single branch-defining peak. Once two excitonic resonances, cross peaks, and transitions into the doubly excited manifold contribute, the largest local maximum is set by interference between several Liouville pathways rather than only by echo refocusing. In the disordered Fig.~4 series, refocusing still biases the frequency-domain maxima toward the rephasing branch. In the homogeneous Fig.~6 series, by contrast, that disorder-refocusing advantage disappears, so waiting-time phase evolution and pathway interference can shift the largest maximum into the nonrephasing branch instead. This interpretation is therefore an inference from the logged amplitudes together with the displayed line shapes, not a separate pathway decomposition.

Among the no-apodization panels in~\autoref{tab:results_dimer_branch_ratios}, the largest absorptive frequency-domain peak occurs in the homogeneous Fig.~6 panel at \(T=\SI{62}{\femto\second}\), with \(\max|\tilde{S}_{\mathrm{abs}}|=9.42\times10^{-2}\). \section{Towards the general \texorpdfstring{$N$}{N}-site extension}
\label{sec:results_nsite_extension}

The numerical workflow developed in this chapter is not restricted to the monomer and dimer. At the level of the implementation described in~\autoref{app:numerical_implementation}, the same sequence remains available for a general $N$-site excitonic model: construct the Hamiltonian and dipole operators in the truncated excitation manifolds, propagate under the three-pulse field, isolate the desired phase-matched contribution by phase cycling, average over static disorder, and Fourier transform the resulting signal into $(\omega_{\mathrm{coh}},\omega_{\mathrm{det}})$ space.

What remains unfinished in the present thesis is not the basic simulation machinery, but the final production of a larger, fully interpreted figure set. A trimer remains the natural next benchmark because it would test the same pipeline in the first system where multiple cross-peak families compete simultaneously while the spectrum is still interpretable by hand.

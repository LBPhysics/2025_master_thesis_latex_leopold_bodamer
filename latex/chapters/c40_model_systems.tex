\chapter{Model Systems}
\label{chapter:model_systems}

This chapter defines the physical models used in the numerical
analyses of this thesis. The open-quantum-system framework was developed
in~\autoref{chapter:oqs_open_quantum_systems}, and the spectroscopy formalism was
developed in~\autoref{chapter:spctr_spectroscopy}. The present chapter
specifies the Hamiltonians, dipole operators, static-disorder model, and
bath-coupling operators used in the simulations of~\autoref{chapter:results}.
For the monomer and dimer, the parameter sets introduced below are chosen to
match benchmark cases studied
in~\cite{mancaletal2006twodimensionalopticalthreepulse,pisliakovetal2006twodimensionalopticalthreepulse}.

The chapter first introduces a general \(N_{\mathrm{sites}}\)-site model. The
monomer and dimer are then obtained as the \(N_{\mathrm{sites}}=1\) and
\(N_{\mathrm{sites}}=2\) limits.

\section{General N-Site Model}
\label{sec:model_ensemble}

In the general case, we consider a system of \(N_{\mathrm{sites}}\) coupled sites. Each site is
locally truncated to a two-level system, so every site can be either in its
ground state or singly excited.

\subsection{Hilbert Space}

For \(N_{\mathrm{sites}}>1\), the Hilbert space is further truncated at the double-excitation
manifold. The resulting basis therefore has three bands:
\begin{itemize}
\item the ground state \(\ket{\mathrm{g}}\),
\item the single-excitation states \(\ket{n}\) with \(n\in\{1,\dots,N_{\mathrm{sites}}\}\),
\item the double-excitation states \(\ket{mn}\) with \(1\le m<n\le N_{\mathrm{sites}}\).
\end{itemize}
Here \(\ket{n}\) means that only site \(n\) is excited, while \(\ket{mn}\)
means that sites \(m\) and \(n\) are excited. Since each site is itself only a
two-level system, a given site can never be excited twice. The
double-excitation sector therefore always consists of excitations on two
different sites.

If only the one-excitation manifold is modelled, the basis is
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{B}_{1\mathrm{ex}} = \left\{ \ket{\mathrm{g}}, \ket{1}, \ldots, \ket{N_{\mathrm{sites}}} \right\},
\end{equation*}
so that the Hilbert-space dimension is \(1+N_{\mathrm{sites}}\). If the two-excitation manifold
is used as well, the basis is enlarged to
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{B}_{2\mathrm{ex}} = \left\{ \ket{\mathrm{g}}, \ket{1}, \ldots, \ket{N_{\mathrm{sites}}}, \ket{12}, \ket{13}, \ldots, \ket{N_{\mathrm{sites}}-1~N_{\mathrm{sites}}} \right\},
\end{equation*}
and the Hilbert-space dimension becomes
\begin{equation*}
\dim \mathcal{H} = 1 + N_{\mathrm{sites}} + \binom{N_{\mathrm{sites}}}{2}.
\end{equation*}


\subsection{Hamiltonian}

In this truncated basis, a generic Frenkel-exciton Hamiltonian reads
\begin{equation*}
\hat{H}_{0}^{(N_{\mathrm{sites}})}
=
\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{sites}}}\omega_{n}\,\ket{n}\!\bra{n}
+
\sum_{m<n}^{N_{\mathrm{sites}}}J_{mn}\,\bigl(\ket{m}\!\bra{n}+\ket{n}\!\bra{m}\bigr)
+
\sum_{m<n}^{N_{\mathrm{sites}}}(\omega_{m}+\omega_{n})\,\ket{mn}\!\bra{mn}.
\end{equation*}
Thus, the diagonal terms contain the site energies and double-excitation
energies, while the off-diagonal terms describe coherent couplings within the
single-excitation manifold.

\subsection{Geometry and Couplings}

The couplings \(J_{mn}\) may be chosen phenomenologically or generated from
transition-dipole interactions. For the latter,
\begin{equation*}
J_{mn} \propto \frac{1}{\lVert \boldsymbol{r}_{mn} \rVert^{3}}
\left[
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
- 3\,
(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{m}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}_{mn})
(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}_{mn})
\right],
\end{equation*}
with site positions \(\boldsymbol{r}_{n}\) and displacements
\(\boldsymbol{r}_{mn}=\boldsymbol{r}_{m}-\boldsymbol{r}_{n}\)
~\cite{griffiths2013introductionelectrodynamics,lehmberg1970radiationatomsystem,masters2014pathsforstersresonance}.


\subsection{Dipole Operator}

The dipole operator connects only adjacent manifolds,
\begin{equation*}
\hat{\mu}^{(N_{\mathrm{sites}})}
=
\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{sites}}}
\Bigl(
\mu_{\mathrm{g}n}\,\ket{\mathrm{g}}\!\bra{n}
+
\mu_{n\mathrm{g}}\,\ket{n}\!\bra{\mathrm{g}}
\Bigr)
+
\sum_{m<n}^{N_{\mathrm{sites}}}\sum_{\ell\in\{m,n\}}
\Bigl(
\mu_{\ell,mn}\,\ket{\ell}\!\bra{mn}
+
\mu_{mn,\ell}\,\ket{mn}\!\bra{\ell}
\Bigr),
\end{equation*}
where the second sum is only needed when the two-excitation manifold is
used. This adjacent-manifold structure provides the dipole channels required
for excited-state absorption in multilevel systems.

\subsection{System--Bath Coupling}

For the open-system treatment, the system operators in the
interaction Hamiltonian~\autoref{eq:oqs_Interaction_Hamiltonian} are modelled to
act locally on the site \(i\) as
\begin{equation*}
\hat{S}_{i}
=
\ket{i}\!\bra{i}
+
\sum_{\substack{m<n\\ i\in\{m,n\}}}\ket{mn}\!\bra{mn},
\qquad i\in\{1,\dots,N_{\mathrm{sites}}\}\,,
\end{equation*}
where the second term is only present when the double-excitation manifold is
retained. Thus the bath associated with site \(i\) acts not only on the
one-excitation state \(\ket{i}\), but also on every double-excitation state
that contains site \(i\).

\subsection{Static Disorder}

Whenever inhomogeneous broadening is included, the relevant electronic energies
are sampled over a static-disorder ensemble before the signal is averaged. In the general \(N_{\mathrm{sites}}\)-site model these fluctuating quantities are the site energies \(\omega_n\). A macroscopic motivation for this construction was given in~\autoref{spctr:sec:macroscopic_polarisation}.
The site environments are modelled to be completely uncorrelated, and their
numerical implementation is documented
in~\autoref{app:sec:inhomogeneous_broadening}.

\section{Monomer as the \texorpdfstring{$N_{\mathrm{sites}}=1$}{Nsites=1} Limit}
\label{sec:model_tls}

The monomer serves as the minimal benchmark system. It contains only one
optical transition and is therefore the simplest case in which the full 2DES
calculation can be tested before coupling, cross peaks, and multilevel
pathways are introduced.

\subsection{Hamiltonian and Dipole Operator}

For \(N_{\mathrm{sites}}=1\), the field-free Hamiltonian and optical dipole operator reduce to
the two-level expressions already introduced
in~\cref{eq:spctr_tls_hamiltonian,eq:spctr_tls_dipole}. For completeness, they
are repeated here:
\begin{equation*}
\hat{H}_{0}^{(\mathrm{TLS})} = \omega_{\mathrm{eg}}\,\ket{\mathrm{e}}\!\bra{\mathrm{e}},
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
\hat{\mu}^{(\mathrm{TLS})}
= \mu_{\mathrm{ge}}\,\ket{\mathrm{g}}\!\bra{\mathrm{e}} + \mu_{\mathrm{eg}}\,\ket{\mathrm{e}}\!\bra{\mathrm{g}},
\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{eg}}=\mu_{\mathrm{ge}}^{*}.
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Phenomenological Lindblad Bath}

For the monomer calibration benchmark, the open-system treatment is kept
phenomenological. In the notation of the general model,
the local excitation projector reduces to
\begin{equation*}
\hat{S}_{\mathrm{e}} = \ket{\mathrm{e}}\!\bra{\mathrm{e}}.
\end{equation*}
The benchmark monomer itself is then coupled to a phenomenological Lindblad
bath through the collapse operators
\begin{equation*}
\hat{S}_{\downarrow} = \sqrt{\gamma_{\downarrow}}\,\ket{\mathrm{g}}\!\bra{\mathrm{e}},
\qquad
\hat{S}_{\uparrow} = \sqrt{\gamma_{\uparrow}}\,\ket{\mathrm{e}}\!\bra{\mathrm{g}},
\qquad
\hat{S}_{\varphi} = \sqrt{2\gamma_{\varphi}}\,\hat{S}_{\mathrm{e}}.
\end{equation*}
The rates \(\gamma_{\downarrow}\) and \(\gamma_{\uparrow}\) describe downward
and upward population transfer, respectively, while \(\gamma_{\varphi}\) is the
pure-dephasing rate. For the benchmark comparison used later, the
zero-temperature limit \(\gamma_{\uparrow}=0\) is chosen. The corresponding
monomer equations of motion are given in~\autoref{app:sec:paper_eqs_explicit_eoms}, see in particular
~\cref{eq:app_monomer_eom_sigma_eg,eq:app_monomer_eom_rho_gg}.

\section{Dimer as the \texorpdfstring{$N_{\mathrm{sites}}=2$}{Nsites=2} Realisation}
\label{sec:model_dimer}

The dimer has two optically active sites and is the smallest coupled system
with coherent excitonic mixing and one double-excitation state \(\ket{\mathrm{AB}}\).
It is therefore the first system in this thesis in which cross peaks and
waiting-time beats appear. In contrast to the monomer, the dimer is
coupled with a microscopic bath.

\subsection{Site Basis and Exciton Basis}

In the site basis \(\{\ket{\mathrm{g}},\ket{\mathrm{A}},\ket{\mathrm{B}},\ket{\mathrm{AB}}\}\), the
field-free Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation*}
\hat{H}_{0}^{(\mathrm{dim})}
=
\omega_{\mathrm{A}}\ket{\mathrm{A}}\!\bra{\mathrm{A}}
+
\omega_{\mathrm{B}}\ket{\mathrm{B}}\!\bra{\mathrm{B}}
+
J\bigl(\ket{\mathrm{A}}\!\bra{\mathrm{B}}+\ket{\mathrm{B}}\!\bra{\mathrm{A}}\bigr)
+
(\omega_{\mathrm{A}}+\omega_{\mathrm{B}})\ket{\mathrm{AB}}\!\bra{\mathrm{AB}},
\end{equation*}
where \(\omega_{\mathrm{A}}\) and \(\omega_{\mathrm{B}}\) are the site energies and \(J\) is the
coherent coupling.

For spectral interpretation, the one-exciton block is diagonalised. The mixing
angle is defined by
\begin{equation}
\tan(2\theta)=\frac{2J}{\omega_{\mathrm{A}}-\omega_{\mathrm{B}}},
\label{eq:dimer_mixing_angle}
\end{equation}
and the one-exciton eigenenergies are
\begin{equation}
\omega_{2,1}
=
\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{A}}+\omega_{\mathrm{B}}}{2}
\pm
\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(\omega_{\mathrm{A}}-\omega_{\mathrm{B}})^2+4J^2}.
\label{eq:dimer_eigen_energies}
\end{equation}
The results are interpreted in the exciton basis, since the Redfield tensor is
evaluated in this basis. The corresponding site-basis and exciton-basis
representation is summarised in~\autoref{fig:rslts_dimer_basis_map}.

    \label{fig:rslts_dimer_basis_map}

In the exciton basis, with \(\ket{0}\equiv\ket{\mathrm{g}}\) and
\(\ket{3}\equiv\ket{\mathrm{AB}}\), the dipole operator is written as
\begin{equation*}
\hat{\mu}^{(\mathrm{dimer})}
=
\sum_{a=1}^{2}
\left(\mu_{0a}\,\ket{0}\!\bra{a}
+
\mu_{a3}\,\ket{a}\!\bra{3}
\right)+
\text{c.c.},
\end{equation*}
with \(\mu_{a0}=\mu_{0a}^{*}\) and \(\mu_{3a}=\mu_{a3}^{*}\). This is also
visually represented in~\autoref{fig:rslts_dimer_basis_map}.
Once the system has been driven into the one-exciton manifold, further
absorption into the doubly excited state becomes possible.

\subsection{Microscopic Bath Coupling}
\label{sec:model_dimer_transfer_model}

For \(N_{\mathrm{sites}}=2\), the site-local coupling operators reduce to
\begin{equation*}
\hat{S}_{\mathrm{A}} = \ket{\mathrm{A}}\!\bra{\mathrm{A}} + \ket{\mathrm{AB}}\!\bra{\mathrm{AB}},
\qquad
\hat{S}_{\mathrm{B}} = \ket{\mathrm{B}}\!\bra{\mathrm{B}} + \ket{\mathrm{AB}}\!\bra{\mathrm{AB}}.
\end{equation*}
The dynamics are generated by the Redfield tensor in~\autoref{eq:oqs_Rabcd_full},
and the explicit exciton-basis equations are listed
in~\autoref{app:sec:paper_eqs_explicit_eoms}.
